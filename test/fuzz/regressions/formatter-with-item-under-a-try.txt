`try h; with "" as d -> h` lost the `;` to a line break, and a top-level
item opening with `with` is then read as the cases of the `try` above it.
The parser reports that as `try ... with`, a form wand does not have.

This is the operator-continuation hazard already guarded there, spelled
as a word instead of punctuation. The item above only has to *end* in a
`try` for it to happen -- `let a = try h` does -- so the guard is on the
item that opens with `with`, not on whatever came before. Brackets say it
is its own statement.
