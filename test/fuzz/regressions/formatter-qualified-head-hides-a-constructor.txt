A constructor takes the bracket written after it, and `guard_constructors`
brackets an argument that would take one it does not own. It reads the
spine that `flatten` gives it, and `flatten` stopped at a module's name.

The parser builds `p.M N` as `App (Qualified (p, M), N)` and `p.M(N)` as
`Qualified (p, App (M, N))` -- the same program, spelled two ways. Only
the first reached the guard as a head and an argument. In the second the
constructor was inside the head, so nothing guarded it:

  p.M(N)(9[])  -->  p.M N (9 [])  -->  (p.M) (N (9 []))

`N` took the bracket that belonged to the spine, which is a different
program, and one that still typechecks -- so only a second formatting pass
disagreeing said anything.

`flatten` now descends through `Qualified` when what it wraps is an
application, and both spellings reach the guard as one head and two
arguments.
