`try h; with "" as d -> h` loses the `;` to a line break, and the `with`
below was then read as the cases of the `try` above it -- reported as
`try ... with`, a form wand does not have.

The hint is for someone writing OCaml, where `try e with cases` is one
statement. It was owed on any following `with` at all, including one
opening a statement of its own. A top-level `with ... as ... -> body` is
an ordinary thing for a script to do: it typechecks, it runs, and the
layout rule every other construct obeys says a line back at the `try`'s
column starts something new. So the hint is now owed only where the
`with` is still part of the same statement -- on the same line, or
indented past the `try`. Both of those still report it.

Fixed here rather than in the formatter. Bracketing every top-level
`with` would have avoided the crash by making a working construct print
as `(with ...)`, which is a worse answer than letting the layout rule
mean what it says.
