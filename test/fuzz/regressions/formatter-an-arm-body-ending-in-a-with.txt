A `match`/`handle` arm body ends only where the next `|` begins, so a bare
`match` printed at the end of one takes the arms below it. `emit_case_body`
writes the bracket that keeps them apart, and it asked whether the body
*ends* in a match by following Let and LetRec tails only. A `with` body is
printed just as unguarded, and this arm was `with k as _ -> match ...`:

    | S!n d k -> with k as _ ->
    match fn () -> d with
    | e -> ""
    | S!e d k -> ()          <- now an arm of the inner match

The handler lost its second operation to the nested match. `If` and `Fn`
print their tails the same way and had the same hole; an `if` with no
`else` carries a bare `Unit` and prints its *then* branch last, which is a
fourth. All four are now followed.

Instability is the symptom, not the bug. The output above is a fixed point
of the formatter in three of the four shapes -- it is simply a different
program, printed without complaint. What made this one report is the
reparse respelling `S!e` as `S! e` once it was read as a pattern rather
than an operation, so a second pass finally disagreed with the first. The
readable shapes are held by `ok_after_format` cases in test_formatter.ml,
each asking for the answer of an arm that comes after the one at fault.
