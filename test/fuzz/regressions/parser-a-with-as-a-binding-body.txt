A `with` could not be a binding's body after a `;`. `is_expr_start` lists
the forms that open an expression where a statement may stand, and it
carried `let`, `if`, `match`, `fn` and -- through `is_atom_start` -- `handle`
and `try`. `with` was in neither.

So `semicolon_joins_the_body` answered no, the `;` ended the item rather
than handing the rest to the body, and the line below fell through to the
file:

  let f () =
    let _ = 1;
    with b as _ -> body

That is the shape the formatter writes for a chain whose body is a `with`,
and it did not parse: "the ';' above ended the definition". The program the
fuzzer started from meant the other reading, and the second pass bracketed
what the first had not.

`with` belongs in `is_expr_start` rather than `is_atom_start`. It opens an
expression the way `let` and `match` do, and like them it is not an argument
without brackets -- `f with r as x -> y` is not an application of `f`.

The hand-writable half is held by a case in test_parser.ml. This file is the
byte sequence the fuzzer reduced to, which nobody would write.
