A binding written inside brackets of its own, as a statement of a block:

  (fn -> t; (let f = fn () -> (let x = 1; 9) in ()))

The brackets are a parenthesis around one expression, so the formatter drops
them. That puts the binding in the block, and the next pass reads it as one
of the block's own and writes the `;`. The two passes disagreed.

This is why the rule cannot live in the parser. The parser sees the
brackets; the printer decides whether to keep them. `items` decides it now:
every binding it reaches is one of the block's statements. Two attempts to
patch the parser's tags site by site left this case, and then left it again.

A `Seq`'s first child is not reached there. That is where a binding written
`in` narrows -- `(let x = 1 in x + 1; 9)` -- and it keeps its `in`.

This does not fail on v0.59.1. The rule arrived in 0.59.2, and so did the
bug.
