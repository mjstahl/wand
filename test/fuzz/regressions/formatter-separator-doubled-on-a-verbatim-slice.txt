An item that opens with an operator continues the item above it, so
`assemble` writes back the `;` that separated the two in the source.
The item above was a verbatim slice, and a slice runs to the next item's
offset -- so it already held that `;`. A second one went on after it,
and the line grew a `;` per pass, for ever.

  ()--
  -let e=();-      -->  -let e=();;   -->  -let e=();;;
  --
  let k=()

`assemble` now asks what the piece above ends with rather than what kind
of piece it is. A pretty-printed item never ends in `;`, so the guard
only ever declines to repeat a separator that is already written.
