A binding with statements above it in the same block takes their `;`. The
retag reached the binding itself and not the bindings inside its body, which
were read as one expression with it and kept their `in`. The next pass saw a
`;` above them and wrote the `;`, so the two passes disagreed:

  let () =            let () =
    match a with  ->    (match a with
    | O -> e             | O -> e);
  in                  ()
  ()

The retag now follows the statement spine: down the last statement of a
`Seq` and down a binding's body. Not into the first child of a `Seq`, which
is where a binding written `in` narrows and keeps it.

Unlike most fixtures here, this one does not fail on the binary before the
commit that fixes it. The rule it tests arrived in that same commit, and the
bug was in the first draft of it. v0.59.1 formats this input stably, because
there it keeps every `in`.
