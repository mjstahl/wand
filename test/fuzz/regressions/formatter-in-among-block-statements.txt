A binding among a block's statements takes the block's `;`. The first
attempt decided that in the parser, from the position the binding was read
at. The parser cannot decide it: the printer drops brackets, so where a
binding ends up is not where it was read.

Here a binding written `in` sat inside a body read as one expression. The
parser retagged the outer binding and not the ones under it, so the first
pass wrote them with `in` and the second, seeing the `;` above them, wrote
the `;`:

  let () =            let () =
    match a with  ->    (match a with
    | O -> e             | O -> e);
  in                  ()
  ()

`items` decides it now. Every binding it reaches is one of the block's
statements, so it takes the `;`. A binding that narrows is a `Seq`'s first
child and is emitted as a statement, which keeps its `in`.

This does not fail on v0.59.1. The rule arrived in 0.59.2, and so did the
bug.
